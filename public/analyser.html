How it works

  1. Reading your statement. CSV files are read column by column. PDFs are turned into text on your device (with Mozilla's pdf.js) and read line by line.
  2. Finding vape purchases. A small machine-learning model, running in your browser, scores each transaction description for how likely it is to be a vape shop or vape brand. Strong matches are ticked for you; possible ones are listed for you to check.
  3. Spend to puffs. Total vape spend ÷ price per device = devices; devices × puffs per device = puffs.
  4. Puffs to cigarettes. The common UK retailer rule of thumb, based on nicotine: a 600-puff device is about 20 cigarettes, so 30 puffs ≈ 1 cigarette.

What this doesn't tell you: health risk. Cigarettes by puffs compares nicotine, not harm. The health risks of smoking even a few cigarettes a day are well measured; the long-term risk of vaping isn't known yet. The Breathe tracker shows what studies find, with sources.

Questions

How many cigarettes is 6000 puffs?

By the 600-puffs-is-20-cigarettes rule, 6,000 puffs is about 200 cigarettes. That rule compares nicotine, not health risk: the long-term risk of vaping hasn't been measured yet, so it can't honestly be turned into a number of cigarettes' worth of harm.

Is my bank statement uploaded?

No. Your browser reads and analyses it. Nothing about it is sent to BigThought or anyone else, and closing the page clears it.

Why didn't it find all my vape purchases?

Statements show the shop, not what you bought. A vape shop is easy to spot; vapes bought with your shopping at a supermarket or newsagent are not. Use "Add a purchase it missed" to tick those.
