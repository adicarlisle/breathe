What happens to your body when you stop

A general guide based on NHS information. Everyone is different, and this is not medical advice.

  1. After 20 minutes your pulse rate starts to return to normal.
  2. After 8 hours oxygen levels recover and the harmful carbon monoxide in your blood halves.
  3. After 48 hours carbon monoxide has left your body, your lungs start clearing mucus, and taste and smell improve.
  4. After 72 hours breathing feels easier and you may have more energy.
  5. After 2 to 12 weeks blood flows better around your body.
  6. After 3 to 9 months coughs, wheezing and breathing problems ease as your lung function improves.
  7. After 1 year your risk of a heart attack is about half that of a smoker.
  8. After 10 years your risk of dying from lung cancer is about half that of a smoker.

Free help to stop: NHS Quit Smoking. People who use stop-smoking support are much more likely to quit for good.

How this is worked out

  • Cost is the price of one cigarette (pack price ÷ cigarettes in a pack) × cigarettes a day × 365.25 days.
  • Life expectancy uses about 20 minutes per cigarette, from University College London's 2024 analysis of long-running UK studies (17 minutes for men, 22 for women). It is an average across many people, not a prediction for you.
  • Interest assumes you save the monthly cost each month, compounded monthly, at the rate you choose.

How much life does one cigarette take?

About 20 minutes on average, according to University College London research published in 2024: around 17 minutes for men and 22 minutes for women.

What is a pack-year?

Smoking 20 cigarettes a day for one year. Doctors use pack-years to describe lifetime exposure: 10 a day for 10 years is 5 pack-years.
